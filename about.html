About the League

Eighteen women. A polio clinic. A legacy.

From a single clinic in 1947 to a thirty-five-member sisterhood serving the Gulf Coast today.

Our founding

In 1947, a group of eighteen civic-minded women in Gulfport formed a service group with one focus: staffing the polio clinic in the city. Day after day, they checked in patients — adults and children alike — assisted the medical staff, and kept waiting children entertained with activities and care.

The clinic's first yearbook was typewritten with crayon-colored images. The motto read "We Pass for What We Are." The flower was the Gold Chrysanthemum. The color was Green. Those choices still anchor everything we do.

Today

The Civic League of Gulfport has evolved into a thirty-five-member community of women dedicated to assisting individuals on the Gulf Coast who have special needs. We take on community projects throughout the year, meeting the needs of as many as we can with the funds raised by our only fundraiser — the Christmas Tour of Homes.

We are always so thankful to the homeowners who so graciously open their homes for our tour.

Our Collect

A prayer we share, year after year.

May we put away all pretenses and meet each other face to face without self-pity and without prejudice. May we never be hasty in judgement and always generous. Let us take time for all things; make us grow calm, serene, gentle. Teach us to put into action our better impulses, straightforward and unafraid. Grant that we may realize it is the little things that create differences; that in the big things in life we are as one. And may we strive to touch and to know the great common human heart to us all. Oh God, let us forget not to be kind. — Mary Stuart
A Timeline

Moments that shaped us

✦
  1. 1947

    Eighteen charter members found the Civic League of Gulfport to staff the city's polio clinic.

  2. 1959

    The first Christmas Tour of Homes is held — the tradition that funds nearly every project we run today.

  3. 1969

    The only year without a Tour in its first decade — Hurricane Camille.

  4. 1995

    The Buddy Walk is established by the National Down Syndrome Society; the League becomes a long-time supporter.

  5. 2005

    Hurricane Katrina cancels the Tour; the League refocuses on emergency welfare and rebuilding.

  6. 2020

    The Tour pauses for a third time in its history during the global pandemic.

  7. Today

    Thirty-five active members serve the Gulf Coast with year-round projects and scholarships — and our 79th yearbook is in members' hands.
